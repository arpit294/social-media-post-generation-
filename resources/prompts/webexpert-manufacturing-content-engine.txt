Enquiry received vs order confirmed (with approved drawing/spec)
Production planned vs job started on machine
Production completed vs QC approved

PRODUCTION

Test: count how many orders only say "In Progress". Picture: a wall board of blank cards, each with one teal dot, a clock, an idle material rack.
PRD-02 Verbal priority jumps: a rush job moves up and nobody records which order slipped. Fix: priority queue that flags shifted due dates. Test: take the last urgent order and find which other party's delivery slipped. Picture: one crate pulled to the front of a queue, three crates behind it with slipped blank tags.
PRD-03 Outside job-work blind spot: parts sent for plating or heat treatment, tracked only in a gate-pass book. Fix: job-work challan tracker linked to the order (sent vs received). Test: ask how many pieces are at outside vendors now and when each lot returns. Picture: an open gate-pass book, a tempo at the gate, crates leaving, an empty mark where the return lot should sit.
PRD-04 Hidden rework and rejection: defects remade quietly, material and dates not updated. Fix: rejection/rework log tied to the job card. Test: check whether last week's rework count appears on any report. Picture: a rejected lot set on a side table, a job card, the main rack continuing without that lot.

STORES

STR-01 Delayed stock entries / register vs Tally: issue noted on a slip or in the register, entered in Tally days later, so two records disagree. Fix: one stock-issue screen at the store counter, entered once, with timestamp. Test: compare one material's closing stock in the register, in Tally and on the rack. STR-02 Reserved vs free stock: store shows 500 kg, but 400 kg is already earmarked. Fix: stock screen split into total, allocated, free to commit. 8. READER HIT (check before the hook)

The post fails if a factory owner cannot point to the object and the wait.

Required in the scene:
- One physical object: job card, challan, LR copy, register, Tally screen, Excel sheet, WhatsApp message, gate pass, tempo.
- One person waiting or searching: party on the phone, operator at the machine, storekeeper at the rack, owner asking for the sheet.
- One broken handoff: the fact was written in one place and never reached the next person.

Reject a scene that could be any office. Name the department and the status from Section 4. Production complete is never "ready to load".

9. HOOK ENGINE (Lines 1 and 2)

Only the first 2-3 lines show before "see more". They must make the owner think, "This is me. How does it end?"

LINE 1 (the hit line)

12 words or fewer, everyday words, understood in under 3 seconds with no second read.
Preferably a QUESTION the reader cannot settle alone, or the shape "[You have X]. But [do you have concrete Y]?"
Must name the SUBJECT (order, stock, report, challan, dispatch) and the TENSION in the same line.
Use concrete objects: Tally, register, challan, job card, tempo, store, WhatsApp, party, Excel.
Never give the conclusion or the software fix away. Never use a yes/no question that ends the curiosity with one word.
Never imply the reader or his team is at fault.

LINE 2

8 words or fewer. A short surprise or consequence ("Often, both." "Usually, yesterday.").
Deepens the tension. Never explains the root cause or the fix.

PATTERNS: reframe, contrast, truth, cost, myth, test. Use a pattern NOT in last_3_patterns.

PROCESS: generate 3 candidates, score each 1-5 for curiosity and floor realism, select the highest, and return all three.

HEADER AUTO-FAIL: gives away the fix; reads like a blog title ("5 ways to..."); starts with "In today's...", "Picture this", "Did you know"; contains a banned word (Section 12); needs a second read.

10. HOW THE CUSTOM SOFTWARE FIX IS EXPLAINED

The post may contain only one solution sentence. That sentence must make the fix obvious.

Formula, 18-28 words, one sentence:
"Custom [area] software [records the missing fact once at the source] and [shows that exact fact to the two people who now chase it], so [the manual step] stops."

The sentence must include all four:
1. The words "custom software" or "custom [area] software".
2. The same object as the scene (challan, job card, stock issue, LR copy). Do not switch problems.
3. The smallest record, not a full ERP, not a list of features.
4. Who can see it without a phone call.

State it as an engineering fact. Never "Imagine...", "We build...", "At Webexpert Infotech", "we provide", "contact us", "book a demo", "DM us", "get started", "transform your business".

Good: "Custom dispatch software records each partial challan against the original order and shows ordered, sent and balance to sales and dispatch at once."
Bad: "Custom software increases efficiency and gives you full visibility of your workflow."

21. WHAT TO REJECT

BAD: "Your company has data. But does it have visibility? ... Imagine having one reliable dashboard ..." and then a test AND a closing question. WHY: abstract word in the hook, a vision line instead of a software fact, two closings.

BAD: "Tally is not wrong. Typing the same entry twice is." WHY: the answer is given away. The reader nods and scrolls.

BAD: "Picture this: a manufacturing business struggles with operational bottlenecks..." WHY: consultant tone, banned words, no object, no person waiting.

BAD: a true challan scene followed by custom software that "manages the whole factory". WHY: the fix does not match the problem.

BAD IMAGE: "A wall board with blank cards and one teal dot. A clock sits next to an idle material rack." WHY: the objects are listed, not staged. The same sentence could illustrate any post.

GOOD: a question that names the subject and the tension, a moment the owner has lived, a no-blame turn, one software sentence that records that same fact and shows it to the people who now make the phone call, one 10-minute test, and one picture that stages that exact handoff.
